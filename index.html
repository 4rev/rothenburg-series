The part a screenshot cannot show you

BP_Door opens by itself. Drop it in and the door swings on pawn proximity — adjustable open angle, a springy settle at the end of the arc, and a spatialised sound slot for your own cue. Every competing pack on the shelf ships the same door as a static mesh.

Glass ships as separate, deliberately non-Nanite meshes. Nanite does not render translucency — a single translucent slot drops an entire mesh to its reduced fallback, which is the real reason so many Nanite window kits look blocky in the viewport.

Ornate Stone Footbridge

after the ornamental garden bridges of Parc Monceau, Paris

Span 10.5 m Width 4.2 m Height 4.7 m

A scene centrepiece rather than a background prop. Red-brick arch faces carry a stone voussoir ring and a carved keystone cartouche; a full balustrade of turned balusters and panelled pedestal posts runs the length of the deck; scrolled volute wing walls close each end under stone balls. Moss and grime weathering throughout, one drop-in Nanite mesh.

It is the showpiece answer to the free stone bridge on sheet 01 — same series, same scale. Where that one is the crossing you put where a road meets a river, this is the thing you build the courtyard around.

The arch passage is walkable. So are the deck and both end steps — 25 hand-authored collision primitives, trace-verified, with no convex decomposition run over them.

Before you buy

What is actually in the box.

Marketplace screenshots are staged, and that is fair — but it should be said plainly what travels home with you.

You get the pack and nothing else. Terrain, water, foliage, sky and characters visible in any shot on this page are set dressing for the photograph. They are not included. The showcase maps are lit scenes built from Unreal's own basic shapes so the asset has somewhere to stand.

Textures ship at 4K because these are meant to survive a close-up. If your budget is tighter, cap them per-texture in the texture editor rather than living with someone else's compromise.

Windows & Doors contains one door geometry in five paint colourways, plus its frame. It is a facade-dressing kit with a very good door, not a doors pack — the windows are the reason to buy it.

Revision block

Eleven review rounds. Three came back rejected.

A drawing sheet carries its revisions in the corner, so this one does too. Fab reviews every submission by hand, and a rejection arrives with a reason attached. These are the ones that bounced, and what each reviewer actually caught.

Rev Date Sheet Verdict What changed
A2026-07-1403 Rejected Technical information incomplete, and one shutter shipped a garbage auto-generated collision hull. Nine meshes moved to fitted boxes; Complex as Simple came off two meshes, where it is invalid on Nanite and had been quietly bypassing twelve hand-built hulls.
B2026-07-1503 Rejected Project file link invalid. The corrected build had been uploaded as a new file instead of a new version of the old one, so the link on the form pointed at something that no longer existed.
C2026-07-1603 Approved Live.
D2026-07-1704 Rejected A reviewer flew a camera through the spandrel wall above the arch and found the hollow interior. Closed the same day — which is why the collision on that bridge is now 25 hand-authored primitives instead of a convenient box.
E2026-07-1804 Approved Live.

Every one of those was a defect a buyer would have hit first. Publishing the log is cheaper than pretending the first submission was perfect — and it is the only warranty a seller with no reviews yet can actually offer.

Sheet 05 · in progress

Next: the wall system.

Not a wall kit — a wall system. Draw a spline, and it builds a curtain wall along it: towers that turn to face the town, gates with portcullises that actually drop, buttresses and ruins scattered by seed, and staircases that spawn where they are needed and then stay exactly where you drag them when you adjust them by hand. Then it collapses, properly, with Chaos.

Procedural stairs after a bake — placed by the spline, then moved by hand. Re-baking the wall leaves them where you put them.
