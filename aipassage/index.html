AI Passage Controller

AI characters take turns at doorways, gates and bridges instead of jamming. This page is the documentation that ships inside the plugin's Docs folder.

AI Passage Controller — Quick Start

What it does: your AI characters take turns at doors, gates, bridges and other narrow passages — one at a time (or as many as you allow), in a fair order, from both directions — instead of jamming in the opening.

What it changes in your game: the passage itself moves nothing. It decides who may go, and when. The optional AI Passage Navigator walks its own NPC through your AI Controller, and in a crowd jam it may nudge other NPCs that carry the navigator — never your player and never any other character. Your Character, AI Controller and NavMesh stay as they are: you swap one move call.

Requirement
Unreal Engine 5.8
Platform Windows (Win64) — the platform it is tested on
Multiplayer not tested, not supported in this version
Navigation a NavMesh where the NPCs walk
NPCs Pawns possessed by an AI Controller. The crowd features (step aside, squeeze past) need a Character

1. Install

From Fab (the usual way):

  1. In the Epic Games Launcher, open your Fab Library, find AI Passage Controller and click Install to Engine (5.8).
  2. Open your project. Edit → Plugins, search "AI Passage", tick AI Passage Controller, and restart the editor.
  3. In the Content Browser click Settings and tick Show Engine Content and Show Plugin Content. The examples are in AI Passage Controller Content → AIPassageController → Examples.

From a zip, into one project: copy the AIPassageController folder into your project's Plugins folder and open the project. If Unreal offers to rebuild the plugin, that needs Visual Studio with C++; a Blueprint-only project cannot rebuild it — use the Fab install instead.

Nothing else. No project settings to change, no subsystem to register.

2. The fastest path — five steps

  1. Drag an AI Passage Controller into your level, in the middle of the doorway.
  2. Add a Trigger Box that spans the opening, floor to head height. Give it the tag AIPassageZone, then in the Outliner drag it onto the controller (that attaches it).
  3. On your NPC Blueprint: Add Component → AI Passage Navigator.
  4. Where your NPC calls Move To Location, call Move To With Passages with the same destination.
  5. Press Play. NPCs whose route goes through that doorway now take turns.

Your NPC does not move yet? From Event BeginPlay call Move To With Passages; make the destination an Instance Editable vector with Show 3D Widget, so you can drag each NPC's goal in the viewport. The level needs a NavMesh Bounds Volume over both sides (in the viewport, Show → Navigation draws the walkable area green).

NPCs still walk straight through? Look in the Output Log for "has no doorway zone": step 2 is missing a part. Selecting the controller in the editor shows the same thing in red.

Section 4 explains each step; section 3 shows it working first.

The Circuit map — 96 NPCs travelling in opposite directions

Narrow and wide passages alternate round the loop: a 130 cm doorway and a 130 cm bridge hold one NPC, and the teams take turns there. The 280 cm stairs and the 240 cm corridor use the Two Way policy: each direction has its own lane, 65 cm right of the centre line, and as many NPCs per direction as fit in single file along it - 5 on the stairs, 6 in the corridor (Max Concurrent Agents, about one per 120 cm of controlled length) - so the teams pass each other without waiting. With 96 NPCs the lines at the narrow passages grow long — up to about 35 waiting — which is the point: that is where taking turns matters. The saved arrangement stays the same on restart.

Right-click orders or Stop take selected NPCs out of the circuit; R resets everyone. 1/2/3 opens/blocks/disables all passages. P stops circuit activity and switches to the existing manual movement comparison; use R to restore the automatic demo. H toggles help. There are already 96 NPCs, so N cannot add more.

The automatic loop is example game-mode behaviour, not a general patrol system. In a crowd a leg can fail (blocked short of the goal); the NPC retries that leg up to three times, with a warning in the log each time. A fourth failure in a row stops that NPC's circuit.

4. Add it to your own NPC

4.1 Place the passage and its doorway box

  1. Drag an AI Passage Controller into your level, in the middle of the doorway or bridge.
  2. Select it. The viewport draws its layout:
    • SIDE A and SIDE B rings — the two entrances;
    • a grey arrow at each waiting line — the direction the LINE EXTENDS, away from the passage;
    • numbered rings A#0, A#1 … — the first few waiting positions;
    • a coloured arrow on each — the direction a waiter will FACE, back towards the door.
  3. Move SideAAnchor and SideBAnchor to the two ends of the narrow space.
  4. Move QueueAAnchor and QueueBAnchor to where each line should start, and rotate them so the grey arrow points AWAY from the passage.
  5. Add a Trigger Box across the opening — floor to head height, the full width of the gap. Tag it AIPassageZone (Details → Actor → Tags), then in the Outliner drag the box onto the controller. The box is how entry and exit are seen: the navigator finds a passage's doorway by that attachment. Until it is attached, the selected controller shows NO DOORWAY ZONE ATTACHED in red.

Put the waiting lines clear of the doorway and clear of the exit lane on the other side. A line that overlaps the opening has people standing in the way of the traffic they are waiting for.

Everything else has a working default.

4.2 The AI Passage Navigator (recommended)

  1. On your NPC Blueprint — your own Character and AI Controller, unchanged — Add Component → AI Passage Navigator. It adds the AI Passage Agent it needs by itself.
  2. Where your NPC called Move To Location, call Move To With Passages with the same destination.

That is the whole integration: one component and one node, plus the doorway box from 4.1.

What the node does for you:

  • plans the route once, finds every passage the route crosses, and approaches each one from the side the route comes from;
  • asks for permission only when the NPC is within Request Distance (600 cm) of the passage, or of the end of the line already waiting there, so a unit ordered from across the map does not start the reservation timeout early, and a late arrival joins the back of the line;
  • waits in its queue place facing the passage, goes through when granted, reports entry and exit, then carries on to the destination — through the next door too;
  • stops the body and goes back to where it asked from if it loses permission before entering, and finishes clearing the opening if it loses it while inside;
  • gives its place in line up if its AI Controller goes away — for example an NPC that dies and is left lying in the line — so the passage does not wait for a body that will never move.

Crowds sort themselves out (Resolve Crowd Jams, on by default). When an NPC is trying to move and getting nowhere because bodies are in the way, the NPCs involved agree who makes way: one holding a passage's permission never does, an idle one always does, otherwise a fixed tie-break. The one making way steps aside — with its neighbours, when they walk the same way — waits a moment, and carries on with its own order. Somebody waiting in a line keeps their place; the others walk round the line. A step aside never enters a passage's opening. With the debug view on you can watch the conversation: "Excuse me", "After you", "Let's make room".

A line moves up like a wave (Line Reaction Time, 0.25 s by default). When the front of a line moves, each waiter steps up a moment after the one ahead of it — its own random share of 0.25 s per place — not all at the same instant. 0 turns it off. One given the door goes at once.

Squeezing past (Squeeze Through Crowds, on by default, needs a Character). When stepping aside has not helped — or at once for an NPC holding a passage's permission — the NPC squeezes past, the way people do in a crowd. For about 1.5 s:

  • its capsule is 5% narrower and lets other pawns overlap it, so overlap events can fire on pawns it brushes;
  • its mesh is 5% narrower and 5% taller;
  • the bodies round it are kept at least 95% of their summed widths apart. Only other NPCs that carry the navigator and are driven by an AI Controller give way — and they take the same 5% shape for a moment. Your player, any character without the navigator, and an NPC holding a passage's permission are never moved, and the squeezer does not push or walk through them. Walled in by such bodies, an NPC keeps trying (in our test, still trying after 90 s);
  • nobody is pushed into a doorway, off a ledge or through a wall.

Everything returns to normal afterwards; if your game changed the capsule, its collision or the mesh scale in the meantime, your value stays. In our dense-crowd test an NPC got out of a ring of 18 standing navigator NPCs in 8.5 s, and the most any of them was moved was 57 cm.

Use the navigator without Unreal's RVO avoidance on the same NPCs. With RVO on, the passage itself still works — in our two-way test all 32 NPCs crossed with no timeouts — but only 20–27 of the 32 then reached their crowded destinations, against 32 of 32 with RVO off: RVO and path following fight each other in a packed group. The others were still walking when the test stopped at 300 s — their orders had not ended, so On Move Finished had not fired. If you keep RVO, give anything that waits for On Move Finished a time limit (in a Behavior Tree, a Time Limit decorator). Not tested with Detour Crowd.

An NPC with the navigator but no order of its own may be nudged up to 1.5 m in a jam. Turn Resolve Crowd Jams off on NPCs your own AI keeps in place (a guard at a post, a vendor). In a corridor about two bodies wide nobody can step aside, and the order fails as it would without this.

Ordering again is safe. Call Move To With Passages as often as your game likes — every click, every squad re-order: while the new route still goes through the same doorway from the same side, the NPC keeps its place in line (and its permission, if it has one). An order to somewhere that no longer needs the doorway gives the place up. For a big group (say 100 units at once), give the orders over a few frames — for example 20 per frame: each order plans a route.

Events on the component:

Event When
On Move Finished (Outcome) once per order: Arrived, Refused, Failed or Stopped. Never from inside the call that gave the order; not at all for an order replaced by a new one
On Obstructing Passage the body is in an opening without permission. Your game decides what the passage does — usually Set Passage Availability (Blocked), counted as in BLUEPRINT_INTEGRATION.md section 8b
On Obstruction Cleared that body has left the opening. Nothing is reopened for you

Stop Passage Move ends an order (it fires On Move Finished with Stopped) and gives up any queue place. Do not issue your own Move To while an order runs; call Stop Passage Move first.

The navigator plans and walks with your AI Controller's Default Navigation Filter Class, the same one Move To Location uses. Waiting NPCs turn to the passage through the lowest AI focus priority (Default), so a focus your game sets always wins; a Character that turns to face its controller (Use Controller Rotation Yaw, or Use Controller Desired Rotation) shows it, one that only orients to movement does not.

4.3 From a Behavior Tree or a State Tree

The built-in Move To task cannot call Move To With Passages. The plugin has a Behavior Tree task that can.

Behavior Tree — add the plugin's task Move To With Passages where your tree used Move To (right-click → Add Task → Move To With Passages). Select it and set its Blackboard Key to your destination: a Vector key, or an Actor key (the actor's location when the task starts; the task does not follow a moving actor). Everything else in the tree stays. The pawn needs the AI Passage Navigator (4.2).

  • It succeeds when the order Arrived. It fails when the order ended Refused, Failed or Stopped, and at once when the pawn has no navigator, the key holds no location, or there is no route.
  • When the tree aborts it (a higher branch takes over, or the tree stops), it calls Stop Passage Move: the NPC gives up its place in line or its reservation.
  • We ran it in a Behavior Tree: the NPC asked, crossed and the task succeeded. Stopped while it waited in line, the NPC gave its place up and the tree stopped at once.

Your own Blueprint version — to change what the task does, build it yourself: a Blueprint class based on BTTask_BlueprintBase, for example BTT_MoveToWithPassages, with one Blackboard Key Selector variable Destination (Instance Editable). 26 nodes, counting each variable getter; we built it from these steps and ran it in a Behavior Tree, which crossed a doorway under control:

  1. Event Receive Execute AI → Get Component by Class (Controlled Pawn, class AI Passage Navigator) → promote the result to a variable Navigator. Every later step uses it.
  2. Bind Event to On Move Finished on Navigator → a custom event PassageMoveFinished (drag from the bind node's red Event pin to create it with the right signature).
  3. Get Blackboard Value as Vector (Destination) → Move To With Passages on Navigator.
  4. Branch on its return value. False (no route, no AI Controller): Unbind Event from On Move Finished on Navigator, then Finish Execute (Success = false).
  5. PassageMoveFinished (Outcome): Unbind Event from On Move Finished, then a Switch on EPassageMoveOutcome: Arrived → Finish Execute (Success = true); every other outcome → Finish Execute (Success = false).
  6. Event Receive Abort AI → Unbind Event from On Move Finished → Stop Passage Move → Finish Abort, all on Navigator. Unbind first: Stop Passage Move fires On Move Finished, and an aborted task must not finish twice.

In the tree, select the task and set its Destination to your blackboard's vector key. Put it where your tree used Move To. Everything else in the tree stays.

State Tree — the same pattern in a StateTreeTaskBlueprintBase task: on Enter State, get the navigator from the context actor, bind On Move Finished and call Move To With Passages; when it fires, unbind and Finish Task (Succeeded = Arrived); on Exit State, unbind and call Stop Passage Move if the order is still running (Is Moving).

Either way: an order replaced by a new Move To With Passages does not fire On Move Finished, so a task that starts a new order must not wait for the old one. (If your game gives the NPC a new order while the plugin's task runs, the task ends when that new order ends.) And if your NPCs use RVO avoidance, an order in a packed crowd may never end (4.2): put a Time Limit decorator on the task.

4.4 The long way — the recipe by hand

Use it instead of the navigator only when you need custom behaviour at a specific step (a different retreat spot, an animation before entering, your own obstruction handling inside the NPC), or your NPC is not a Pawn with an AI Controller. It uses the AI Passage Agent component directly: Add Component → AI Passage Agent. The component never moves your NPC.

Open /AIPassageController/AIPassageController/Examples/Blueprints/BP_PassageDemoNPC and read its Event Graph. It is meant to be copied into your own graph.

Be honest about the size of it. The graph is 214 logic nodes in 13 commented blocks. Six of those blocks are the integration; the rest are the demonstration — the fault scenarios behind keys 0 and 4 to 9, and the layout that makes the example readable. A minimal integration really is small. A safe one is the six blocks below, and none of them is optional.

The six blocks that are the integration
  1. Ask. Call Request Passage on the component, with the passage and the side you are coming from. Choose the side from the route your NPC is walking — do not guess it from whichever anchor happens to be nearest.
  2. Read the refusal properly. Rejected does not always mean nothing was created — see 4.5. Check Has Active Passage Relationship before you walk away from anything.
  3. Move when told. Handle On Queue Slot Changed and On Passage Granted. Both mean "where you should be has changed". Both do the same thing: call Get Suggested Movement Target and move to the result.
  4. Report entry and exit from the body, not from a timer. In the example a trigger volume spans the doorway. Begin-overlap reports entry; end-overlap reports exit — and only once the capsule is nearer the far side, because backing out the way you came is not a traversal.
  5. Handle losing permission — all four ways of losing it. On Passage Failed covers the two timeouts and invalidation. Cancellation does not fire it; it arrives on On Passage State Changed as Cancelled. 5.3 is about why skipping that one is dangerous.
  6. Go somewhere sensible afterwards — and which somewhere depends on where the body actually is. Through: a parking spot beyond the passage. Still on the approach side: a retreat spot behind it. Standing in the opening: tell your game, then clear the opening. 5.2 and 5.3 explain why these must never be mixed up.

Five events and one movement call, plus the branch in 6. That is the whole integration — and the branch is the part people leave out.

When an NPC dies. If the body stays in the world, call Cancel Current Passage on its agent component in your death logic, so its place in line or its permission is released at once. A destroyed NPC is cleaned up by itself.

Copying it into an NPC that already has a graph

This order was rehearsed on a separate project whose NPC had its own Character, its own AI Controller and its own Begin Play route. That NPC's graph went from 7 nodes to 191, and it compiled with no errors. Three from each side then crossed under control.

  1. Add the component and keep its default name, AIPassageAgent. The pasted events bind to that name.
  2. Create the variables first. Copy them from BP_PassageDemoNPC's variable list: the eleven in BLUEPRINT_INTEGRATION.md section 4, plus the six demonstration ones. Paste before they exist and every getter is an error.
  3. Select all in the demo's Event Graph, copy, and paste into yours. Expect errors of the form "In use pin … no longer exists on node StartPassage. Please refresh node". The rehearsal got 45 of them. Run File → Refresh All Nodes. It clears them and keeps the links.
  4. If your NPC already has Event BeginPlay, the pasted one is not pasted. Unreal turns it into a custom event named CustomEvent that nothing calls. It compiles, but the NPC never asks for the passage and stands still. Connect your own Begin Play to the Branch that reads StartOnBeginPlay, at the point where your route used to start walking. Then delete the CustomEvent node. The same engine rule applies to Event ActorBeginOverlap and ActorEndOverlap if your NPC already handles them: feed block 9 from your existing handler, and keep your own initialization and overlap logic.
  5. Delete block 11's nodes, and keep its six variables. The comment boxes are headings above their blocks, not frames around them. So select the nodes from the heading "11." down to the end of the graph, not the box. See BLUEPRINT_INTEGRATION.md section 3 for why the variables stay: keep all six, with the five Simulate… flags false.
  6. Change your route. Where your NPC used to walk to its destination, it now asks. That old destination becomes ParkingSpot. Put RetreatSpot on the NPC's own side.
  7. DemoRecovery is typed to BP_PassageDemoControls. Retype it to whatever opens and closes your doors, and re-point its BeginObstruction and EndObstruction calls. Or leave it empty: the NPC still clears the doorway, and only the blocking is skipped.
  8. Compile, then place the passage and its doorway box (4.1).
  9. Set the placed NPC instances. Assign Passage; set EntrySide from each NPC's intended route; put ParkingSpot beyond the passage and RetreatSpot safely on its approach side. Set StartOnBeginPlay true for this first test, or call StartPassage from your route logic. Connect DemoRecovery to your obstruction handling if you use block 8. Confirm the copied defaults, including MaxIdleRedrives = 6 and all five Simulate… flags false.

4.5 Two things that will bite you on the long way

Bind before you ask. Request Passage answers immediately, and if the way is clear it fires On Passage Granted before it returns. A handler bound afterwards misses the grant. Events added to a component in a Blueprint are bound before Begin Play, so the example is safe; code that binds by hand must bind first.

Do not move on both. Because the grant event has already fired, the Granted return value needs no movement of its own. Use the events to drive movement, and the return value only to notice that you were refused.

Rejected does not always mean "nothing was created". It has two causes and they need opposite responses:

Why it was rejected What exists afterwards What you must do
A fresh request was turned away nothing go somewhere else
You asked for a second passage, or the opposite side of the one you are already using the original relationship, untouched nothing. Carry on with it.

Walk away in the second case and you abandon a reservation the passage is still counting, and strand everybody queued behind you. The only safe test is to ask Has Active Passage Relationship after the call. Unavailable and Invalid do always mean nothing was created.

5. Four things that are easy to get wrong

5.1 Exit means the body is clear

Report exit when the capsule has left the opening, not when it has reached a point on the far side. An NPC's origin can pass the goal while its back half is still filling the gap; hand the next agent a doorway in that state and they collide in it.

The example reports exit from the end of the overlap with the doorway volume. A Delay node cannot do this — a delay is a guess.

Leave room between the far anchor and the volume. A move finishes once the pawn is within its acceptance radius plus its own radius of the goal. If that stopping point is not comfortably past the point where the volume releases, the NPC halts while still overlapping and never reports exit at all — it then holds the passage until the traversal timeout. With a margin of two centimetres, half the NPCs in our tests did exactly that.

5.2 A refused NPC must not walk to the far side

This one is not obvious and it matters.

If an NPC is refused, or loses its reservation before getting in, and you then send it to a destination beyond the passage, navigation will happily route it through the doorway it was just refused — with no permission, possibly into oncoming traffic.

The passage cannot stop that. It controls permission, not collision. Nothing in the plugin can prevent a body that simply walks through.

So the sample keeps two destinations and never mixes them:

Variable Used when Where
ParkingSpot it actually came through, or is inside and must finish clearing beyond the passage
RetreatSpot refused, or lost the reservation while still on the approach side the side it approached from

The navigator (4.2) does this for you.

5.3 Losing permission does not stop the body — you have to

This is the one that costs people a bug report.

Cancel Current Passage takes the permission away. The Move To you issued earlier is still running. Cancel an NPC that is walking towards the doorway and it keeps walking, straight through the opening it no longer has any right to use, while its own state reads Cancelled.

Measured in our tests: cancelled at x = −950 cm, observed inside the doorway with no permission, and out the far side at x = +183 cm.

Cancellation is not a failure event. The component deliberately does not fire On Passage Failed for it — you asked for it, so you already know. It arrives on On Passage State Changed with NewState = Cancelled, and if nothing is listening there, nothing stops the body.

So the sample sends every way of losing permission — refusal, cancellation, reservation timeout, traversal timeout — to one handler, which does three things in this order:

  1. Stop the current movement. Everything decided afterwards is worthless while an old move order is still carrying the NPC somewhere else.
  2. Ask where the body actually is — Is Overlapping Actor against the doorway volume it remembered when it first touched it. Not a flag.
  3. Then choose: in the opening → tell the game and finish clearing; already across → carry on to parking; still on the approach side → retreat.

Do not use a "did I report entry" flag for step 2. The sample keeps one, ReportedInside, and deliberately does not decide on it. It records what the passage was told, which after a missed exit report or an entry-side retreat is exactly the thing that is wrong. Reversing an NPC that is already across walks it back through the doorway with no permission — the same bug, pointing the other way.

5.4 Clearing an obstruction is not permission to reopen

When a body is stranded in the doorway, something has to shut the passage until it is clear — see section 7. The tempting next step is to reopen it once the body is out. Do not infer that you are allowed to.

A flag saying "I was the one who blocked this", plus a check that the passage still reads Blocked, is not ownership. An enum value cannot say who set it. Another system asking for Blocked, or a Disabled that somebody later changed to Blocked, look exactly the same from inside your recovery.

Measured, with a real body timing out in the doorway and the game then making its own decision:

Game's decision, made while the body was stuck At real capsule clearance
Disabled held correctly
Blocked overwritten to Available, and a fresh NPC admitted
Disabled, then Blocked overwritten to Available, and a fresh NPC admitted

It was also nearly invisible. Checking at the moment of clearance passed, because the NPC admitted through the reopened doorway timed out a moment later and set Blocked again.

So the sample announces the clearance and stops. Reopening is an explicit decision — key 1 in the example, Set Passage Availability in your game. Leaving a passage shut is always safe; reopening one is a decision about the whole game.

6. Navigation prerequisites

The plugin needs none. Your project does:

  • a NavMesh Bounds Volume covering the area, and navigation built;
  • your NPC reachable from its waiting position, and the far side reachable from the doorway;
  • an AI Controller possessing the pawn. On a Character Blueprint set Auto Possess AI to Placed in World or Spawned — "Placed in World" alone silently skips anything spawned at runtime, and then nothing moves at all.

A waiting position that lands off the NavMesh is an authoring problem. The passage computes where people should stand from the anchor you placed; it does not check that the spot is walkable. The navigator reports a waiting place it cannot reach in the log.

7. Timeouts are not decoration

Two settings on the controller decide how it recovers from an NPC that stops cooperating:

  • Reservation Timeout Seconds (default 15) — permission was given and the NPC never arrived.
  • Traversal Timeout Seconds (default 20) — it got inside and never came out. On a long bridge or corridor, make it longer than the slowest NPC needs to cross.

Setting either to 0 disables that timeout. An NPC that then stalls holds the passage forever and everybody behind it waits forever. Leave them on unless you have a specific reason.

Queueing itself has no timeout. A queued agent waits until it is granted, cancelled or destroyed (a navigator NPC also gives its place up when its AI Controller goes away).

A timeout reclaims permission, not space. It takes the reservation back so the queue can move; it cannot move a body. If the NPC that timed out is still standing in the opening, the passage will hand permission to the next agent and they will walk into it. Deciding what to do about that is your game's job — BP_PassageDemoControls shows one way, and key 8 runs it.

Blueprint Integration

The recipe from QUICK_START.md, in detail. Read the graph in /AIPassageController/AIPassageController/Examples/Blueprints/BP_PassageDemoNPC alongside this — the comment blocks there are numbered to match the sections below.

Scope of this page: the normal workflow. Everything advanced is in section 7, deliberately after the point where you already have something working.

Want it without the graph? The AI Passage Navigator component runs blocks 1–10 of this page in C++: add it and call Move To With Passages instead of Move To Location (QUICK_START.md, "The short way"). Block 8's report becomes its On Obstructing Passage event; what the game does about it is still yours, exactly as in 8b. Read on when you need to change a step.

1. What each side owns

Owner Responsibility
AI Passage Controller who may enter, in what order, in which direction, and taking back permission that was abandoned
AI Passage Agent (on your NPC) one NPC's relationship with one passage, and answering "where should I go now"
Your AI actually walking there

The plugin never calls MoveTo. That is not a limitation to work around, it is the reason the component drops into an existing project without changing anything.

It also does not intercept your existing MoveTo calls. A passage request is something your route asks for explicitly. If your NPC walks through a door without asking, the passage does not know it is there.

2. The graph, block by block

Block 1 — Ask

Event BeginPlay ──▶ Branch (StartOnBeginPlay) ──▶ StartPassage

StartPassage ──▶ AIPassageAgent ▸ Request Passage (Passage, FromSide)
                        │
                        └─▶ Switch on EPassageRequestResult
                                Granted      ─ (nothing: the event already ran)
                                Queued       ─ (nothing: the slot event already ran)
                                Rejected     ─┐
                                Unavailable  ─┼─▶ HandleRefusal
                                Invalid      ─┤
                                Cancelled    ─┘

Choose FromSide from the route the NPC is walking. Not from the nearest anchor: an NPC approaching diagonally can be nearest the wrong end, and the passage will then plan its traversal in the wrong direction.

The Granted and Queued branches are deliberately empty. Request Passage broadcasts On Passage Granted or On Queue Slot Changed before it returns, so movement has already started. Adding a MoveTo here as well sends two move orders for one instruction, and the second cancels the first.

Block 2 — A refusal is not always a loss

This distinction matters, and it is easy to get wrong.

Rejected has two causes that need opposite responses:

Cause What survives
A fresh request was turned away (One Way from the forbidden side) nothing at all — no queue entry, no reservation, no reference
A second request while a relationship is already live — a different passage, or the opposite side of the same one the original relationship is untouched and still running

Unavailable and Invalid behave like the first row. But because the second row exists, the only safe test is whether a relationship survived the call:

HandleRefusal ──▶ Branch (AIPassageAgent ▸ Has Active Passage Relationship)
                     True  ─ (nothing: an earlier relationship is still live)
                     False ─▶ RetreatToApproachSide

Walking away in the second case would abandon a reservation the passage is still counting and strand everybody queued behind it.

None of these results is a retry hint. Where nothing was created, send the NPC somewhere else; do not retry in a loop.

Block 3 — Move whenever the passage says so

On Passage Granted     ──▶ DriveToCurrentTarget
On Queue Slot Changed  ──▶ DriveToCurrentTarget

Both events mean the same thing: where you should be has changed. Handling them identically is what keeps the graph small.

On Queue Slot Changed fires when the NPC first gets a place in the line and every time its position in that line changes. It does not fire because the passage actor was moved — the positions are computed when you ask, so re-read them if your door itself moves.

Block 4 — Ask where to go, then go. No completion callback.

DriveToCurrentTarget ──▶ AIPassageAgent ▸ Get Suggested Movement Target
                              │
                              └─▶ Branch ──▶ Get AI Controller ▸ Move To Location
                                                  │
                                                  └─▶ Switch on result
                                                          Failed ──▶ HandleUnreachableTarget
                                                          (others: nothing)

Get Suggested Movement Target answers for whatever the NPC is doing right now:

State Target
Queued that NPC's own waiting position
Reserved / Entering / Traversing / Exiting the anchor on the far side
anything else nothing, and it returns false

Never cache it.

This graph does not subscribe to move completion, and that is deliberate.

When the queue shuffles forward, the previous move is cancelled and reports failure a moment later. A handler cannot tell that obsolete report apart from a current one, because Blueprint has no way to compare AI move-request identity — the id inside FAIRequestID is a private property with no Blueprint accessor, and GetCurrentMoveRequestID is not exposed at all. Any handler would be guessing, and a wrong guess cancels a reservation the NPC is actively using.

Listening to nothing is the only answer that is always right.

A route that genuinely cannot be walked is caught at issue time instead, from Move To Location's immediate Failed result — once, with no counter and no retry budget.

Block 4b — A movement check, bound to the work

ArmMovementCheck    ──▶ Set Timer by Function Name ("KeepMoving", 0.5s, looping)   -- from block 4
DisarmMovementCheck ──▶ Clear Timer by Function Name ("KeepMoving")

KeepMoving (every 0.5s)
   └─▶ Branch (Has Active Passage Relationship)
          False ──▶ DisarmMovementCheck
          True  ──▶ Get AI Controller ▸ Get Move Status
                       Moving ──▶ IdleRedriveCount = 0
                       Idle   ──▶ IdleRedriveCount + 1
                                    └─▶ Branch (> MaxIdleRedrives)
                                           True  ──▶ HandleUnreachableTarget
                                           False ──▶ DriveToCurrentTarget

A move can still be abandoned without anyone being told: another agent bumps this one, the path is rebuilt, the request is aborted. With no completion callback there is nothing to notice that.

So while this NPC owns a relationship, twice a second: if its controller has gone idle, ask where to go and set off again. Already at the goal answers AlreadyAtGoal and costs nothing.

This matters in practice, not just in theory. Without it, measured, NPCs stopped one centimetre short of clearing the doorway and sat there for the full traversal timeout.

This is not a retry loop, and three things keep it from becoming one:

  • it is armed in block 4, when the passage first gives this NPC somewhere to be — not on Begin Play. An NPC with nothing to do is not polling;
  • it clears itself the moment the relationship ends, and both destinations in block 6 clear it too;
  • it gives up. MaxIdleRedrives re-drives that all end idle is not a slow route, it is a route that is not going to be walked, and block 5 deals with it.

Standing still is not being stuck. A re-drive that answers AlreadyAtGoal resets the counter, because an NPC waiting in its own queue slot is idle every single time this looks at it — and it is idle because it is doing exactly what it was told. Measured, without that reset: every queued NPC counted six idle polls of correct waiting, decided its route was unwalkable, and cancelled. All three NPCs on the second side ended up back at their retreat spots having never entered the doorway once.

Block 5 — A route that cannot be walked

HandleUnreachableTarget ──▶ Is Overlapping Actor (PassageZone)?
                               True  ──▶ ReportObstruction            -- block 8
                               False ──▶ Cancel Current Passage       -- block 7b does the rest

Move To Location answers Failed immediately, so this is decided once. It is also where block 4b ends up when a move has stopped making progress.

bAllowPartialPath is off on the passage move. Left at the engine default it is on, and navigation then accepts a route that stops short and answers RequestSuccessful — so an unreachable target looks exactly like a reachable one and the NPC walks confidently to nowhere with nothing to report.

Where the body is decides what happens. The simple design cancels and retreats here regardless. That is right for an NPC that never got in — it gives its place up so everybody behind it moves, and goes back the way it came. It is wrong for an NPC standing in the opening: reversing drags a body back through the doorway the wrong way, into oncoming traffic, with no permission.

Notice what the False branch does not do: it does not also issue a retreat. Cancelling fires On Passage State Changed, block 7b stops the movement and chooses a destination, and that is the cancellation contract being obeyed rather than described.

Block 6 — Two destinations, and they are not interchangeable

ContinueToParking     ──▶ Move To Location (ParkingSpot)   -- the FAR side
RetreatToApproachSide ──▶ Move To Location (RetreatSpot)   -- the side it came from

This is the most important thing on this page.

ParkingSpot is beyond the passage. An NPC is only sent there once it has actually come through, or once it is already inside and has to finish clearing the opening.

RetreatSpot is on the side it approached from. A refusal, or a reservation lost while the body is still on the approach side, goes here.

Both events clear the movement check from block 4b first: this is the end of the NPC's business with the passage either way.

Partial paths are allowed on these two moves, unlike the passage move in block 4. Getting as close as navigation can manage is a perfectly good outcome when nothing depends on arriving exactly, and nothing is gated on the result.

Sending a refused NPC to the far side asks navigation for a route to the other side of the wall — and the shortest one goes straight through the doorway it was just refused, with no permission, possibly into oncoming traffic. The passage cannot stop that: it controls permission, not collision.

Block 7 — Finished, or lost it

On Passage Exited        ──▶ ReportedInside = false ──▶ ContinueToParking

On Passage Failed        ──▶ RecoverAfterLosingPermission

On Passage State Changed ──▶ Switch (NewState)
                                Cancelled ──▶ RecoverAfterLosingPermission

On Passage Failed carries a reason: reservation timeout, traversal timeout, or invalidation.

An explicit cancellation is not a failure and does not fire it. You asked for it, so the component tells you through On Passage State Changed instead. If nothing is listening there, nothing stops the body — see block 7b.

Block 7b — Losing permission: stop the body, then look at where it is

RecoverAfterLosingPermission
   ──▶ DisarmMovementCheck
   ──▶ StopCurrentMovement          (Get AI Controller ▸ Stop Movement)
   ──▶ Is Overlapping Actor (PassageZone)?
          True  ──▶ ReportObstruction                      -- a real body in a real opening
          False ──▶ ReportedInside = false
                     └─▶ nearer ParkingSpot than RetreatSpot?
                            True  ──▶ ContinueToParking     -- already across
                            False ──▶ RetreatToApproachSide -- still on its own side

Stopping the movement is first, and it is the whole point of this block. Losing permission does not stop a body. In our tests, a sample that called Cancel Current Passage and left the old Move To running did exactly that: a cancelled NPC was observed inside the doorway with no permission and out the far side at x = +183 cm, its own state reading Cancelled the whole way.

Then the destination is chosen by measurement, not by a flag.

Where the body is What happens Why not the obvious thing
in the opening tell the game (block 8), then finish clearing reversing drags it back the wrong way
already across carry on to ParkingSpot retreating walks it back through the doorway with no permission — the same bug, mirrored
still on its own side RetreatSpot —

ReportedInside is deliberately not consulted here. It records what the passage was told. After an entry-side retreat, or an exit that was never reported, that is exactly the thing that is wrong. The doorway volume and the two destinations are measurements; the flag is a memory of a message. The sample keeps it, and keeps it out of this decision, on purpose.

Why compare against the NPC's own two destinations rather than the passage anchors? Because Get Traversal Target and Get Approach Target need a live relationship, and by the time this runs there is not one — they answer with a zero vector, every distance becomes a tie, and a tie sends an NPC that is already across back through the doorway.

Block 8 — A body in the doorway with no permission

If a reservation expires while its NPC is physically inside, the passage will admit the next agent into an opening that is still occupied. The scheduler cannot see bodies and cannot clear one. It issues permission, nothing more.

So the game has to act — and not the stuck NPC itself:

ReportObstruction ──▶ Branch (Obstructing)
                        True  ──▶ ContinueToParking            -- already reported; keep clearing
                        False ──▶ Obstructing = true
                                   └─▶ DemoRecovery ▸ BeginObstruction
                                        └─▶ ContinueToParking

ReportClearance   ──▶ Branch (Obstructing)                     -- from block 9, on real clearance
                        True ──▶ Obstructing = false
                                  └─▶ ReportedInside = false
                                       └─▶ DemoRecovery ▸ EndObstruction

The obvious design has each NPC write Blocked on the way in and Available on the way out, unconditionally. We tested it: both halves are wrong, and both were reproduced:

  • the reopen erased a Disabled decision the game had made while the NPC was still stuck, and admitted the next agent;
  • the reopen fired on the first body to get out, while a second was still standing in the opening.

An NPC knows it is obstructing. It does not know what the passage is for. So it reports, once, and something that can see the whole picture decides. Clearance is reported from the doorway volume in block 9, when the capsule has actually left — not when the NPC thinks it has.

Block 8b — What the game does with that report

In the example that something is BP_PassageDemoControls:

BeginObstruction ──▶ ObstructionCount + 1
                      └─▶ Switch (Passage ▸ Availability)
                             Available ──▶ Set Passage Availability (Blocked)

EndObstruction   ──▶ ObstructionCount − 1 (never below zero)
                      └─▶ Branch (count <= 0)
                             True ──▶ announce that the doorway is clear

Two rules:

  1. Count, do not toggle. The passage stays shut until the last body is clear. Otherwise the first NPC to get out reopens a doorway a second one is still standing in.
  2. Clearing an obstruction does not reopen anything. It announces the clearance and stops. Reopening is a separate, explicit decision — key 1, or your own Set Passage Availability.

Why rule 2 is not laziness

A tempting fix keeps a DemoBlockedPassage flag and reopens if the flag is set and the current value still reads Blocked. That looks like ownership and is not. An enum value cannot say who set it. Another system asking for Blocked, or a Disabled that somebody later changed to Blocked, are indistinguishable from this actor's own closure.

Measured against that version, with a real body timing out in the doorway and the game then applying its own closure:

Game's decision At real capsule clearance
Disabled held correctly
Blocked overwritten to Available, 1 fresh reservation admitted
Disabled, then Blocked overwritten to Available, 1 fresh reservation admitted

Worse, it was nearly invisible: checking only at the moment of clearance passed, because the NPC admitted through the reopened doorway timed out under the shortened traversal timeout and set Blocked again before anything looked. The example's automated tests now sample the invariant on every frame from the external decision onwards, which is the only reason it stays fixed.

Leaving a passage shut is always safe. Reopening one is a decision about the whole game, and a demo NPC recovery is the last thing that should be making it.

Blocking is non-destructive: everybody waiting keeps their place and their turn, so this costs the queue nothing but time.

In your project this is not new code. It is whatever already opens and closes your doors. Set Passage Availability is the entire seam, and the plugin needs no manager, no subsystem and no registration to make this work.

Block 9 — Report where the body actually is

ActorBeginOverlap ──▶ Branch (Actor Has Tag "AIPassageZone"
                              AND Other Actor ▸ Get Attach Parent Actor == Passage)
                          └─▶ Branch (Passage ▸ Has Reservation (Self))
                                  └─▶ Notify Entered Passage ──▶ ReportedInside = result

ActorEndOverlap   ──▶ (same identity test)
                          └─▶ PassageZone = Other Actor
                                └─▶ Branch (Passage ▸ Has Reservation (Self))
                                        True  ──▶ ConfirmExitIfClearOfFarSide
                                        False ──▶ ReportClearance

The volume is also REMEMBERED, on both ends, before the reservation is checked. PassageZone is what block 7b asks "is this body in the opening right now", and an NPC can be physically in a doorway it has no permission for — which is the whole situation block 8 exists for.

No reservation and the capsule has left the box means the OPENING IS CLEAR. That is the only fact ReportClearance needs, and it is measured rather than remembered. The handler itself does nothing unless this NPC actually reported an obstruction, so the ordinary case costs one branch.

ReportedInside is deliberately kept out of every occupancy decision: it records what the passage was told, which after a missed exit or an entry-side retreat is exactly the thing that is wrong.

The volume is identified by attachment, not by the tag alone. A tag is shared by every passage zone in the level, so a neighbouring doorway's volume would otherwise report an entry — or a completed traversal — against the passage this NPC is actually using. Attaching each volume to its own passage answers "whose doorway is this" with no new API.

Entry also requires an actual reservation. A queued NPC brushing the volume has no permission and has not entered anything.

Block 10 — Which way did it leave?

ConfirmExitIfClearOfFarSide
   ──▶ distance to Get Traversal Target  <  distance to Get Approach Target
          True  ──▶ Notify Exited Passage ──▶ ReportedInside = false
          False ─ (it backed out the way it came; leave the reservation alone)

Overlap ending only says the capsule is clear of the box. An NPC that backs out the way it came has also stopped overlapping, and reporting that as an exit would tell the passage a traversal finished while the NPC is still on its original side.

Give the far-side anchor room. A move finishes once the pawn is within its acceptance radius plus its own radius of the goal. If that stopping point is not comfortably past the point where the volume releases, the NPC halts while still overlapping and never reports exit at all. In the example the anchor is 360 cm out and the volume releases at 124 cm — a margin of 142 cm. At an earlier 220 cm the margin was two centimetres, and measured, half the NPCs hung there until the traversal timeout.

SimulateUnreachableTarget key 7: send the next move somewhere navigation cannot reach
SimulateStallInside key 8: stop dead in the opening

They exist so every failure path on this page can be reproduced on a key press instead of waited for. Blocks 4, 9 and 10 still read them; at false they change nothing.

Keys 0 and 4 are the two halves of "lost a reservation", and they are not interchangeable.

Key 0 Key 4
What is suppressed the movement the entry notification

Correct recovery retreat carry on to ParkingSpot

Key 4 does not demonstrate an unauthorised crossing. The NPC holds its reservation the whole way across; only the report is missing. Sending it back would be the unsafe thing.

5. Traffic settings

One setting matters; the rest have working defaults.

Traffic Policy

Policy When both sides are waiting
First Come, Direction Lock (default) the side whose front waiter has waited longest goes next
Alternating strict turn-taking between sides
Batch like Alternating, but up to Batch Size agents per contested turn
One Way only One Way Entry Side is accepted; the other side is refused outright
Two Way (wide passages) nobody waits for the other side: each direction has its own lane (Two Way Lane Offset to the right of travel) and its own Max Concurrent Agents

Choose it at design time. Changing it while traffic is moving is not corrupting, but the turn already running is not retro-fitted.

Max Concurrent Agents — how many may hold permission at once. Above 1 means several agents travelling the same way. Opposite directions never mix, at any capacity (Two Way excepted: there the limit is per direction, in separate lanes). Raising it does not let one side monopolise the passage: once the other side is waiting, the current turn stops admitting new agents and drains before the direction changes.

6. Availability — a door that opens and closes

One property, three states, one setter:

State Fresh requests Existing queue New grants
Available accepted kept yes
Blocked accepted, answered Queued kept no
Disabled refused with Unavailable kept no

Use Blocked for a door that is shut right now. Agents queue, walk to their waiting places, and go the moment it reopens — with no new request from them. Nobody asks twice, nobody polls the passage, and nobody loses their place. That is the point of it being a separate state from Disabled.

To be precise about what the sample does and does not do: it never re-issues a request. It does re-issue a move when its own controller has gone idle (block 4b), which is a different thing — that is the NPC checking whether it is still walking, not asking the passage again.

Use Disabled to take a passage out of service, so approaching AI is told immediately to go somewhere else.

Changing availability is never destructive. Nobody is cancelled, failed, reordered or evicted, in either direction.

At runtime, always use Set Passage Availability. The Details-panel field sets the STARTING state and is deliberately locked while the game is running: writing it directly would change what the passage reports without re-running the scheduling that lets the waiting agents move, which produces a passage that says "Available" while nobody goes anywhere.

This is also the entire integration seam for a door, gate, lift or Nav Link system. When your route closes, call Set Passage Availability (Blocked). When it opens, call it with Available. The plugin never touches your Nav Link.

7. Optional, and deliberately out of the basic recipe

Notify Entering Passage / Notify Exiting Passage — extra phases for projects that want to distinguish "walking to the door" from "in the doorway", or "on the way out" from "clear". They are inspection detail. The basic recipe reports entry and exit only, and that is a complete lifecycle.

Controller-global events (On Request Queued, On Passage Granted, On Passage Released, On Request Cancelled, On Queue Order Changed, On Reservation Phase Changed) — these broadcast about every agent and you would have to filter by requester yourself. They are for something watching the whole passage: a counter above a gate, an analytics hook. An NPC binds its own component's events instead, which are the same information already filtered.

Pump Scheduler — maintenance, run automatically by the controller's own timer. There is no reason for game code to call it.

Queue-slot primitives (Get Queue Slot Transform, Get Queue Assignment, Get Queue Index) — the machinery under Get Suggested Movement Target. Useful for tooling; not needed to walk through a door.

8. Performance notes

  • No Tick on the controller and none on the agent component.
  • The optional navigator ticks only while it squeezes through a crowd (about 1.5 s at a time). Measured with 120 NPCs jammed at one door: on average 0.04–0.05 ms per squeezing NPC per frame, under 2 ms in the worst frame (editor Play-in-Editor, Development build, a laptop Ryzen 7 5800HS; not measured in a packaged game). Each order costs one synchronous path query per order and per passage crossed, plus one box test per passage per path segment — so give a big group's orders over a few frames. Its 0.5 s check timer runs only while the NPC is approaching or crossing a passage, and a 0.4 s crowd-jam check runs while it has an order.
  • One timer per controller, armed only while it has somebody queued or inside, and disarmed when idle.
  • Waiting positions are computed when asked, never stored. A fifty-agent queue allocates nothing.
  • The debug overlay is off by default, costs nothing when off, and is compiled out of Shipping builds entirely.

Troubleshooting

Start here: select the passage and tick Show Debug Visualization (AI Passage → Debug), then press Play. The overlay names the state in words, not only in colour, and most of the problems below are answered by reading it.

No words, only shapes? The default Debug Text is On Hover: words appear only next to a visible mouse cursor. A game that hides the cursor (most first- and third-person games) needs Debug Text = Always — the log says so after the cursor has stayed hidden for 10 seconds. Always needs the player to have a HUD; the log says so too if there is none.

It draws:

  • availability, with a sentence explaining what that state means;
  • reservations held against capacity, and the current direction;
  • both queue lengths, with each waiting position numbered and labelled;
  • the phase of every agent currently holding permission;
  • DRAINING when the passage is deliberately emptying before a direction change;
  • DISABLED WITH n STILL WAITING when a shut passage is holding a queue;
  • the last reservation to end and why, for a few seconds after it happens;
  • a short speech bubble over an NPC for 2.5 s whenever its state changes: Waiting (#2), My turn!, Going through, Through!, Door shut - waiting, Refused - ..., Left the line, Gave up my turn, Too slow - turn lost, Stuck inside - timed out. Drawn every frame on the viewport (no HUD needed), following the NPC; hidden when Debug Text is Off.

The overlay only reads. Turning it on cannot change behaviour.

Nothing moves at all

Work down this list. It is ordered by how often each one is the answer.

1. Is there a NavMesh? Press P in the viewport. No green means nothing can walk anywhere. Add a NavMesh Bounds Volume over the area and build navigation.

2. Is the NPC possessed? On the Character Blueprint, Auto Possess AI must be Placed in World or Spawned. "Placed in World" alone silently skips anything spawned at runtime: no AI Controller, so every MoveTo does nothing and the passage gets blamed.

3. Did anything actually ask? The plugin does not intercept your existing MoveTo calls. A passage request is an explicit call on the route. If the overlay shows waiting A:0 B:0 and no reservations, nobody asked.

4. Is the passage Available? The overlay says BLOCKED or DISABLED in words if not.

5. Is the waiting position reachable? Waiting positions are computed from the queue anchor you placed. Nothing checks that they land on the NavMesh. Move the anchor.

An NPC waits and never goes

The overlay says What it means What to do
BLOCKED temporary closure; requests queue, nobody is admitted call Set Passage Availability (Available)
DISABLED WITH n STILL WAITING out of service, and the old queue was kept on purpose reopen it, or cancel those agents explicitly
DRAINING working correctly — the agents inside are finishing so the other side can have a turn nothing. This is not a deadlock
reservations 1/1 and someone else queued the passage is full nothing, unless the holder is stuck (below)
everything looks idle and open see "Nothing moves at all"

DRAINING is the one worth learning. A passage that is deliberately holding back before changing direction looks exactly like a locked one if you only count reservations.

An NPC holds the passage and never comes out

That is what the two timeouts are for.

  • Reservation Timeout Seconds (default 15) — it was given permission and never arrived. The overlay reports last release -> Name: TimedOut.
  • Traversal Timeout Seconds (default 20) — it got inside and never reported exit. Reported as TraversalTimedOut.

If either is set to 0, that timeout is off, and such an NPC holds the passage forever. Setting them to 0 is the single most common way to build a permanent deadlock with this plugin.

The two reasons are worth telling apart:

  • TimedOut — never entered. Usually the route to the doorway is broken, or the NPC's own logic dropped the move.
  • TraversalTimedOut — entered and never exited. Usually the exit notification is missing: it physically left, but nothing told the passage.

An NPC finished but the passage still thinks it is inside

Your exit reporting is not firing. Check, in order:

  1. The doorway volume has the tag the recipe looks for (AIPassageZone in the example).
  2. The NPC's capsule has Generate Overlap Events ticked.
  3. The volume overlaps pawns rather than blocking or ignoring them.
  4. You are reporting on overlap END, not on reaching a target point.

Point 4 matters even when everything looks right: an NPC's origin can pass its goal while its back half is still in the gap. Report exit from the body leaving the opening.

If nothing reports exit at all, Traversal Timeout Seconds will eventually reclaim the capacity — that is the safety net, not the mechanism.

Two NPCs get wedged in the doorway

The passage grants permission, and permission is not a collision volume. If two bodies are in the opening at once, one of them did not ask, or one entered without permission.

  • Every NPC using the passage must request it. One that walks through without asking is invisible to the scheduler.
  • Check Max Concurrent Agents. Above 1 deliberately admits several agents — travelling the same way, but they still have to fit.
  • Check the opening is actually wide enough for one capsule with room to spare. The example uses 120 cm for Unreal's default 68 cm-wide capsule.

An NPC crossed the passage without permission

Two causes, and the second one catches almost everybody.

1. Your cleanup path sent it there

If a refused NPC — or one that lost its reservation before entering — is told to go to a destination on the far side, navigation routes it through the passage it was just refused. The scheduler cannot stop this: it issues permission, it does not own collision, and it never sees a body that simply walks through.

Keep two destinations and never mix them:

Situation Send it
Came through, or is already inside and must finish clearing beyond the passage
Refused, or lost the reservation while still on the approach side back to the side it came from

The sample calls these ParkingSpot and RetreatSpot.

2. You cancelled it and nothing stopped the body

Cancel Current Passage removes the permission. It does not stop the Move To you already issued. The NPC keeps walking on its old order, through the opening it no longer has any right to use, while its own state reads Cancelled the entire way.

Measured in an earlier build of the shipped example: cancelled at x = −950 cm, observed inside the doorway with no permission, and out the far side at x = +183 cm.

And cancellation does not fire On Passage Failed. That event is for outcomes — the two timeouts and invalidation. A cancellation is a decision you made, so the component reports it on On Passage State Changed with NewState = Cancelled. If you only bound the failure event, nothing in your graph ever hears about it.

What to do:

  1. Handle On Passage State Changed and act on Cancelled.
  2. Stop the movement first — Get AI Controller ▸ Stop Movement. Everything you decide afterwards is worthless while an old move order is still carrying the NPC.
  3. Only then choose where to send it, from where the body actually is — not from a flag recording what you told the passage earlier. See BLUEPRINT_INTEGRATION.md, block 7b.

Press 4, 7 or 8 in the Quick Start map to watch this go right.

An NPC reaches the far side but never reports exit

It stopped short of clearing the volume.

A move finishes once the pawn is within its acceptance radius plus its own capsule radius of the goal. If your far-side anchor is close to the volume that reports exit, the NPC can halt while it is still overlapping — and then it holds the passage until the traversal timeout, every time, on some fraction of crossings.

Work it out:

volume releases at   = volume half-depth + capsule radius
the NPC stops at     = anchor distance - (acceptance radius + capsule radius)

The second number must be comfortably larger than the first. In the shipped example it is 266 against 124, a margin of 142 cm. At an earlier 220 cm anchor the margin was 2 cm and half the NPCs hung there.

Entry or completion is reported on the wrong passage

Two doorways near each other, and the sample's volume test is too loose.

A tag alone is shared by every passage zone in the level, so an NPC brushing a neighbouring volume will report entry — or a finished traversal — against the passage it is actually using.

Associate each volume with its own passage. The example attaches the trigger to its passage actor and checks Get Attach Parent Actor as well as the tag.

Also require an actual reservation before reporting entry: a queued NPC touching the volume has no permission and has not entered anything.

An NPC gives up as soon as the queue moves

This is the classic integration bug, and it is worth recognising.

When the queue shuffles forward, the waiting NPC's current MoveTo is cancelled, and it reports failure a moment later. If your move-failure handler cancels the passage, it throws away a reservation the NPC is actively using.

Blueprint cannot tell that obsolete report apart from a current one. The id inside FAIRequestID is a private property with no Blueprint accessor and GetCurrentMoveRequestID is not exposed, so any handler is guessing — and a counter shared by every callback does not fix that, it just hides it.

The sample's answer is to not subscribe to move completion at all. Nothing obsolete can arrive if nothing is listening. Instead:

  • a route that genuinely cannot be walked is caught at issue time, from Move To Location's immediate Failed result;
  • a move abandoned mid-way is picked up by a half-second poll: if the passage still expects something of this NPC and its controller has gone idle, ask where to go and set off again.

Polling steady state is safe where listening is not — there is no in-flight abort to race with.

Without that poll, measured, NPCs stopped one centimetre short of clearing the doorway and sat there for the full traversal timeout.

A request is refused immediately

Result Meaning
Rejected this component already has a live relationship with a different passage or the opposite side; or this is a One Way passage and you asked from the forbidden side
Unavailable the passage is Disabled, or something is shutting down
Invalid the passage, the actor or the side was not usable
Cancelled a listener cancelled the request from inside its own callback

None of these is a retry hint.

Unavailable, Invalid and Cancelled create nothing — no queue entry, no reservation, no reference. Send the NPC somewhere else.

Rejected is the exception, and getting it wrong strands your queue. It has two causes:

  • a fresh request was turned away — nothing exists, go elsewhere;
  • you asked for a second passage, or the opposite side of the one you are already using — and that leaves the original relationship completely untouched.

Walk away in the second case and you abandon a reservation the passage is still counting, and everybody queued behind you waits for an NPC that is never coming. Always check Has Active Passage Relationship after a refusal before you send the NPC anywhere.

Note that Blocked does not refuse. It answers Queued and holds the request until the passage reopens, so nobody has to ask twice and nothing polls the passage.

One-way traffic refuses the side I expected

One Way Entry Side names the side traffic comes FROM. Side A means A → B. A request from the other side is refused with Rejected and creates nothing at all — it does not even queue.

Switching a passage to One Way does not evict agents already waiting on what has just become the forbidden side.

The Availability field is greyed out during Play

That is deliberate. The Details field sets the starting state; at runtime, availability must change through Set Passage Availability, because that call is what re-runs scheduling and releases the waiting agents.

Writing the field directly would change what the passage reports without moving anybody — a passage that says "Available" while its queue stands still, which is the exact deadlock this plugin exists to prevent.

Stop play and the field is editable again.

Something is physically blocking the doorway

The scheduler cannot clear an obstruction, and it will not pretend to.

If a body, a crate or a frozen NPC is sitting in the opening, the passage will keep granting permission to agents who then cannot get through. Timeouts will reclaim their reservations, and the queue will keep cycling. Nothing recovers, because nothing in the plugin can move the obstacle.

That is game logic. Detect it, move it, destroy it, or call Set Passage Availability (Blocked) until the way is clear.

Timeouts reclaim OWNERSHIP, not space. They are the answer to "an agent abandoned its permission", never to "the doorway is full of furniture".

BP_PassageDemoControls shows one way to own this, and both of its rules are worth copying:

  1. Count the obstructions; do not toggle a flag. The passage stays shut until the last body is clear. Otherwise the first NPC to get out reopens a doorway a second one is still standing in.
  2. Clearing the obstruction does not reopen anything. Announce that the way is clear and stop. Reopening is a separate, explicit decision.

Rule 2 usually surprises people, so see "the passage is stuck Blocked" below before you change it. Press 8 in the Quick Start map to watch both rules do their job.

The passage is stuck Blocked and nothing gets through

First: in the shipped example this is not a bug. Press 1.

When a body is stranded in the doorway the demo blocks the passage, and when the body is clear it says so and stops. It does not reopen. That is deliberate, and it is the correction for a real defect — see below.

If it is your own code, check in this order:

  1. Read the Availability on the controller. If it says Blocked, this is not a scheduler problem — the scheduler is doing exactly what it was told.
  2. Find what set it. Turn on Verbose Logging; every Set Passage Availability is reported.
  3. Did your clearance path ever run? A block applied when a body got stuck is released by a matching clearance. If the block is raised on an NPC event but the clearance is raised on a physical overlap ending, an NPC destroyed or teleported in between never reports it and the count never comes back down. The sample guards this with an Obstructing flag so the two are always balanced, and clamps its count at zero so a stray clearance cannot drive it negative.

Why the sample will not reopen for you

Because it cannot tell whether the closure in front of it is still its own.

A "the block is mine" flag plus a check that the passage still reads Blocked is not ownership. An enum value cannot say who set it. Measured, with a real body timing out in the doorway and the game making its own decision while it was stuck:

Game's decision At real capsule clearance
Disabled held correctly
Blocked overwritten to Available, and a fresh NPC admitted into the doorway
Disabled, then Blocked overwritten to Available, and a fresh NPC admitted

If your own recovery reopens automatically, this is the bug you have, and it will be hard to see: checking at the moment of clearance passed, because the NPC admitted through the reopened doorway timed out a moment later and set Blocked again.

Leaving a passage shut is always safe. Reopening one is a decision about the whole game.
